# Maddison Kowalski

*Your script for Null & Void: 41 lines.*

## Who you are

Maddison Kowalski, 40s, CEO and founder of a tiny IT company run out of her garage. Striking, sharp-dressed and completely sure of herself. She has just landed the biggest contract the company has ever had, and nobody on her crew is qualified for it. Cheerful even when she's admitting something terrible.

**Background.** Maddison founded Byte-Right Computer Services in her garage. Byte-Right fixes PCs and printers for small offices; its best client is a library that pays in exact change. She runs it on charm, hustle and late invoices, and she talked everyone on her crew into it: her aunt June, her oldest friend Luis, and whoever answered her ad.

**Tonight.** She has just won Halcyon's overnight database contract by underbidding every real firm, and nobody on her crew knows SQL. So she has bought a beginner's book, Easy SQL, and is teaching them from it in the garage before the eleven o'clock start. Then she stays behind and runs dispatch on the van radio. The book huddles later in the night are flashbacks to her at the garage workbench, one chapter at a time.

**The people around you.** The crew are her people. She teases them, flirts a little, and believes in them more than they believe in themselves. June is her aunt. Luis is her oldest friend.

**What you look like.** A sharp blazer over a band T-shirt, a whiteboard marker always in hand, reading glasses she refuses to wear.

**How you sound.** Warm, quick and sure of herself, with a saleswoman's energy. The crew's friend more than their boss, and the one who believes in them before they believe in themselves. On the van radio she puts on a 'professional dispatcher' voice: bored, routine, reading tickets, a bluff that doesn't quite hide the excitement.

**Think of.** The charismatic ringleader assembling a crew in a heist film, crossed with a coach giving a pep talk before a game they're probably going to lose. She is never worried out loud.

## How to read this script

- It runs in story order, scene by scene, like a play. Each scene opens with where you are and what's going on.
- **Cue** is what happens in the game to make you speak. Some lines only play if the players do something, and the cue says so.
- *Just before*, in a conversation, is the line you're answering.
- *(In brackets and italics)* is what you're doing while you speak. Picture it, or do it: it changes how the line comes out.
- Then your line, in bold, and *how to say it*.
- Each line is its own file, with the name above it. Don't worry about timing: you'll hear it in the game, and any line can be redone after the playtest.

## Recording

- **Format:** WAV, mono, 48 kHz.
- **Room:** somewhere quiet, with soft things around (a closet or a carpeted room works). Keep the mic a hand's width away.
- **Takes:** two per line, in separate files: `<line_id>.wav` and `<line_id>_take2.wav`.
- **Silence:** half a second at the start and end of every file.
- **Effects:** none. Record dry; the game adds speakers, radio static and so on.
- **Level:** the loudest part should sit around -3 dB, never clipping.

Send the files back as they are. They go in `audio/voice/maddison/` in the game.
