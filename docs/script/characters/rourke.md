# Dale Rourke

*Your script for Null & Void: 77 lines.*

## Who you are

Dale Rourke, the building's facilities manager, 50s. Tonight he's escorting an IT crew around after hours, and he's friendlier than he needs to be.

**Background.** Dale has run facilities at Halcyon Tower for years: the doors, the lights, the boilers, the badge readers. He is the man everyone calls when something breaks, and he likes being that man. He knows every shortcut, every sticky door, and which vending machine eats quarters, and he tells the same building trivia to every visitor because people like it. He never mentions the director by name.

**Tonight.** The director has asked him to escort an outside IT crew around the tower after hours. Dale knows what the director really wants from them, and the crew doesn't. So on the ground floor and the server floor he is the perfect host: he needs them to trust him. On the security floor, in Secure Lab B, he stops pretending.

**The people around you.** The crew are guests he's been told to look after, and he's good at it. He jokes with them, covers for them and buys them coffee. He talks about WARDEN, the building's computer, like an old coworker he doesn't quite trust. Dr. Vale is a missing colleague he'd rather not discuss.

**What you look like.** Halcyon facilities polo under a windbreaker, a lanyard heavy with keys and badges, a clipboard under one arm.

**How you sound.** Warm, folksy, a little too friendly: a tour guide who has given this speech a hundred times. At the betrayal the warmth drains out but he never shouts; he sounds tired, not evil. In the fight: short, winded, through his teeth. He laughs once, in the lab: the only time his warmth turns into real glee.

**Think of.** The friendly neighbour in a thriller who turns out to be in on it. Play the first half as a decent man doing a favour, not as a villain in disguise. The betrayal works because you meant every kind word on the way up.

## How to read this script

- It runs in story order, scene by scene, like a play. Each scene opens with where you are and what's going on.
- **Cue** is what happens in the game to make you speak. Some lines only play if the players do something, and the cue says so.
- *Just before*, in a conversation, is the line you're answering.
- *(In brackets and italics)* is what you're doing while you speak. Picture it, or do it: it changes how the line comes out.
- Then your line, in bold, and *how to say it*.
- Each line is its own file, with the name above it. Don't worry about timing: you'll hear it in the game, and any line can be redone after the playtest.

## Recording

- **Format:** WAV, mono, 48 kHz.
- **Room:** somewhere quiet, with soft things around (a closet or a carpeted room works). Keep the mic a hand's width away.
- **Takes:** two per line, in separate files: `<line_id>.wav` and `<line_id>_take2.wav`.
- **Silence:** half a second at the start and end of every file.
- **Effects:** none. Record dry; the game adds speakers, radio static and so on.
- **Level:** the loudest part should sit around -3 dB, never clipping.

Send the files back as they are. They go in `audio/voice/rourke/` in the game.
