# Marcus Bell

*Your script for Null & Void: 105 lines.*

## Who you are

**Marcus Bell, 40s.** Former mall security guard. Steady, cautious and observant. Checks corners. Knows exactly how night guards think, and hates it here. He walked a shopping mall's corridors at night for ten years and knows exactly how a night guard thinks. He swore he was done with night work, and here he is.

**The crew.** You're part of Byte-Right Computer Services, a tiny IT company run out of Maddison Kowalski's garage. Nobody on the crew can code. Everyone has one practical skill, and Maddison talked each of you into this job.

**Tonight.** Halcyon Data Systems has hired Byte-Right for an overnight database fix in Halcyon Tower, a 30-storey research tower outside town. The job starts at eleven. You're learning SQL from a beginner's book on the way, and things go wrong fast. Your crew lines are you thinking out loud: noticing things, getting stuck, getting it. Later in the night you find more books and read them aloud together.

**You and the others.** Checks corners, counts exits, notices what's out of place. The steady one, who hates every minute of this.

**Think of.** The veteran cop in a thriller, days from retirement, who knows something is wrong before anyone else does.

**How you sound.** Yourself, as your character: muttering to the crew, not performing. Quick and natural. Never sounds like a hint; it's somebody remembering where they saw something.

**Your name.** Players type their own name, so nobody in the game calls you Marcus, and none of the lines below says anyone's name.

## How to read this script

- It runs in story order, scene by scene, like a play. Each scene opens with where you are and what's going on.
- **Cue** is what happens in the game to make you speak. Some lines only play if the players do something, and the cue says so.
- *Just before*, in a conversation, is the line you're answering.
- *(In brackets and italics)* is what you're doing while you speak. Picture it, or do it: it changes how the line comes out.
- Then your line, in bold, and *how to say it*.
- Each line is its own file, with the name above it. Don't worry about timing: you'll hear it in the game, and any line can be redone after the playtest.

## Recording

- **Format:** WAV, mono, 48 kHz.
- **Room:** somewhere quiet, with soft things around (a closet or a carpeted room works). Keep the mic a hand's width away.
- **Takes:** two per line, in separate files: `<line_id>.wav` and `<line_id>_take2.wav`.
- **Silence:** half a second at the start and end of every file.
- **Effects:** none. Record dry; the game adds speakers, radio static and so on.
- **Level:** the loudest part should sit around -3 dB, never clipping.

Send the files back as they are. They go in `audio/voice/crew_marcus/` in the game.
